# Queue cards — 0.16.0

When the waiting roster changes, the bot reads the channel's five most recent public messages. If the current queue card is among them (including fifth), it edits that message in place. Otherwise it posts the updated card at the bottom, records the new message ID, then deletes the previous card. Other people's messages are never deleted. Private ephemeral messages are not part of channel history.

The same rule applies to explicit `/queue` refreshes. **A match start is different:** the bot first publishes the check-in/match card, then posts a fresh queue card underneath and retires the old queue card only after the replacement succeeds. The queue is empty when everyone waiting entered the match; overflow and players joining during delivery stay on the next queue. This works for automatic and manual starts, including uneven teams. Repeated Join or Leave clicks do not duplicate a player or publish another card. Successful **Join and Leave button** clicks receive a silent Discord acknowledgement; the public roster is the feedback. `/add`, `/remove` and other public updates clear their temporary slash response after success. Rejected actions still explain the problem privately. See the [complete interaction feedback review](interaction-feedback.md) for the retained private replies.

Enabled queues with an existing published card also check public history **every 15 minutes**, even without player activity. Cards in positions one through five are left completely untouched. Buried or deleted cards are recreated through the same durable delivery path: post successfully, save the receipt, then delete the old card. Only the bot's tracked queue card is retired. No notification mentions or private confirmations are sent. This is a periodic check, not a guarantee that a busy chat can never push a card below fifth place between checks.

In the settings website, open the queue's **Queue controls and access** section and turn off **Keep queue card near the bottom** to stop periodic checks. It defaults to on, including older configurations that omit `keepQueueVisible`. Turning it off does not change Join/Leave or explicit `/queue` refresh behavior. Paused, unpublished, removed and read-only recovery queues are not polled. Undelivered periodic replacements are cancelled when the setting is disabled; an HTTP send already accepted by Discord may finish. Normal player updates remain deliverable.

Each community uses its existing Durable Object alarm, with one persisted deadline shared across its queues and one history lookup per channel per check. No new Gateway connection, cron scan or continuously running timer is added. Objects can hibernate between work. Existing communities adopt the schedule on their next wake; a newly published card gets its first check 15 minutes later. The optional [voice preview](voice-preview.md) keeps its independent five-minute refresh. Its unchanged lists cause no extra edits. The bot needs View Channel, Read Message History, Send Messages and Embed Links in the queue channel.

The durable outbox coalesces pending roster changes and retains its selected message ID across edit retries and restarts. History errors retry without deleting the old card or assuming it is too old. A missing card is recreated. Uncertain creates are reconciled before cleanup, and stale edit retries cannot overwrite the latest roster on a shared message. Rate limits and finite retry limits still apply; after repairing a blocked permission, `/queue` retries the saved publication.

Periodic history errors, invalid responses and timeouts preserve the card and wait for the next 15-minute interval. Discord route/global rate limits are shared with all other sends. A queue with unfinished or blocked publication work is skipped instead of resetting its retry budget. The delivery path rechecks history before replacing a buried card, because a concurrent update may already have moved it. With more than five queue cards in the same channel, all cannot fit in the last five; use separate channels or disable automatic visibility on lower-priority queues.

Regression coverage lives in `tests/queue-recency.test.ts` and the signed-request tests in `tests/player-interactions.test.ts`: positions one/five/six, scheduled checks, shared-channel reads, setting persistence/cancellation, replacement ordering, edit and create receipt loss, restart recovery, deleted cards, history outages and rate limits, stale work, match formation, silent joins/leaves and private errors. Discord is mocked in these tests; actual client rendering requires a human button click.

Queue cards no longer have a Community Games footer. Optional voice previews replace that space with an indented italic list, without an extra heading. A **Ping** button posts a separate invitation to eligible voice participants with a Join button; it has a shared five-minute cooldown and no private success receipt. They are off until explicitly enabled in a queue’s Voice presence settings.

The visibility deadline is disposable DO key-value state (`queueVisibilityDue`), reconstructed after restart if absent. An additive `queue_publications.visibility_only` column identifies scheduled work so disabling the setting can cancel it without dropping player updates. Existing rows default to zero. SQL backups include the column; older backups restore with its default, and recovery copies remain read-only. Existing queue membership, matches, ratings and wallets are unchanged by deployment. Prefer a forward repair; before downgrading, drain pending visibility work and remove the new setting from saved configurations through a supported configuration change. Older binaries do not honor cancellation of scheduled work and cannot import backups containing the new column.

## Cost estimate for 50 communities

The 15-minute interval assumes **one monitored channel per community**, one shared bot installation and a 30-day month:

- `50 × 30 × 24 × 4 = 144,000` periodic channel checks per month (148,800 in 31 days).
- Unchanged channels need one Discord GET and no message writes. Queues in the same channel share that GET. A replacement adds a fresh history check, POST and DELETE; normal interactions and recovery retries are additional.
- Routine polling uses about 288,000 DO invocations including the shared rate-gate reservation. If every check replaces one card, approximately 864,000 invocations cover checks, delivery alarms and reservations, before rate-limit/retry overhead. Both fit the paid plan's one million included requests.
- At an assumed **1–3 seconds of total active DO wall time per channel check**, including delivery work when needed, duration is `144,000 × (1–3) × 0.128 = 18,432–55,296 GB-seconds/month`. This is an estimate, not a measured production latency or billing statement. Objects hibernate between checks; network waits count while work is active, and outbound HTTP calls have five-second timeouts.

With otherwise available allowances, the periodic feature fits within the **$5/month Workers Paid baseline**, below the requested $10. SQLite reads/writes and these small records are expected to fit the included allocations at this scale. Multiple channels, high traffic, slow APIs, retries and unrelated account usage increase consumption. This is not a hard spending cap.

**The complete existing app cannot be promised below $10 solely by choosing this interval.** One continuously open outbound voice connection uses about 331,776 GB-seconds/month; the AoE2 feed adds its own duration. Two such connections running continuously already exceed the account-wide 400,000 GB-second allowance. Cloudflare rounds excess duration up to million-GB-second billing units at $12.50 each, so even a small overage can put the total above $10. Account for those services separately and review actual usage; no billing monitor or automatic game shutdown is introduced here.

Pricing checked 27 October 2026 (Pacific): [Durable Object pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/) and [Workers pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/). The included allowances are shared across the account.

## Match-start ordering and preparation settings

Each match-start queue publication stores its required match IDs in the additive `queue_publications.after_matches` column. Existing rows and older restored backups default to `[]`. Queue replacements wait for recorded match-message receipts, including when several matches start before delivery. New joins coalesce into the waiting publication without losing this ordering. A blocked match publication blocks its dependent queue publication without an idle retry loop; recovering the match card wakes the queue publication. Lost receipts retain the existing nonce and reconciliation behavior. Back up before deployment; older binaries cannot import backups containing the additional column. No player, match, rating or wallet tables change.

In **Website → Queues → your queue**, set **Check-in time limit (seconds)** (1–3600) and save. The **Require check-in** toggle remains independent. Turn off **Enable map selection / voting** to skip all map voting and picking for new matches; map pools and preferences remain saved for later reuse. These settings do not change a match already in progress. When the optional map vote-as-check-in setting is enabled, its map selection timer controls that combined stage, as labelled in the form.
