# Queue cards — 0.16.0

When the waiting roster changes, the bot reads the channel's five most recent public messages. If the current queue card is among them (including fifth), it edits that message in place. Otherwise it posts the updated card at the bottom, records the new message ID, then deletes the previous card. Other people's messages are never deleted. Private ephemeral messages are not part of channel history.

The same rule applies to explicit `/queue` refreshes. **A match start is different:** the bot first publishes the check-in/match card, then posts a fresh queue card underneath and retires the old queue card only after the replacement succeeds. The queue is empty when everyone waiting entered the match; overflow and players joining during delivery stay on the next queue. This works for automatic and manual starts, including uneven teams. Repeated Join or Leave clicks do not duplicate a player or publish another card. Successful **Join and Leave button** clicks receive a silent Discord acknowledgement; the public roster is the feedback. `/add`, `/remove` and other public updates clear their temporary slash response after success. Rejected actions still explain the problem privately. See the [complete interaction feedback review](interaction-feedback.md) for the retained private replies.

Enabled queues with an existing published card also check public history **every 15 minutes while the queue is in use**: from any join, leave, `/queue` refresh, card publication, or match start, change or finish, until **6 hours** after the last of these. A queue nobody has used for 6 hours is not checked at all; the next join, leave or command publishes its card again as usual, and checks resume 15 minutes later. Cards in positions one through five are left completely untouched. Buried or deleted cards are recreated through the same durable delivery path: post successfully, save the receipt, then delete the old card. Only the bot's tracked queue card is retired. No notification mentions or private confirmations are sent. This is a periodic check, not a guarantee that a busy chat can never push a card below fifth place between checks.

In the settings website, open the queue's **Queue controls and access** section and turn off **Keep queue card near the bottom** to stop periodic checks, including while the queue is in use. It defaults to on, including older configurations that omit `keepQueueVisible`. Turning it off does not change Join/Leave or explicit `/queue` refresh behavior. Paused, unpublished, removed and read-only recovery queues are not polled. Undelivered periodic replacements are cancelled when the setting is disabled; an HTTP send already accepted by Discord may finish. Normal player updates remain deliverable.

Each community uses its existing Durable Object alarm, with one persisted deadline shared across its queues and one history lookup per channel per check. No new Gateway connection, cron scan or continuously running timer is added. Objects hibernate between work, and an idle community has no alarm at all. After deployment, existing queues are checked again from their next activity; a newly published card gets its first check 15 minutes later. The alarm handler re-arms its alarm once, after its work, and Discord requests release their five-second timeout as soon as Discord answers, so a check costs one short invocation rather than a doubled one held open for the timeout. The optional [voice preview](voice-preview.md) keeps its independent five-minute refresh. Its unchanged lists cause no extra edits. The bot needs View Channel, Read Message History, Send Messages and Embed Links in the queue channel.

The durable outbox coalesces pending roster changes and retains its selected message ID across edit retries and restarts. History errors retry without deleting the old card or assuming it is too old. A missing card is recreated. Uncertain creates are reconciled before cleanup, and stale edit retries cannot overwrite the latest roster on a shared message. Rate limits and finite retry limits still apply; after repairing a blocked permission, `/queue` retries the saved publication.

Periodic history errors, invalid responses and timeouts preserve the card and wait for the next 15-minute interval. **A channel Discord keeps refusing** (403 or 404 for its history or deliveries, three times in a row spanning at least an hour, for example after the bot was removed) is no longer checked, and its pending deliveries are held as `channel_unreachable` without contacting Discord. The setup records' delivery diagnostics show "The bot can't reach #channel. Re-add the bot or fix its permissions, then save the queue." Saving the settings, any successful interaction or delivery in that channel clears the state and retries the held and refused deliveries once. Discord route/global rate limits are shared with all other sends. A queue with unfinished or blocked publication work is skipped instead of resetting its retry budget. The delivery path rechecks history before replacing a buried card, because a concurrent update may already have moved it. With more than five queue cards in the same channel, all cannot fit in the last five; use separate channels or disable automatic visibility on lower-priority queues.

Regression coverage lives in `tests/queue-recency.test.ts`, `tests/alarm-budget.test.ts` (idle-day and active-evening alarm budgets, activity window, refused channels) and the signed-request tests in `tests/player-interactions.test.ts`: positions one/five/six, scheduled checks, shared-channel reads, setting persistence/cancellation, replacement ordering, edit and create receipt loss, restart recovery, deleted cards, history outages and rate limits, stale work, match formation, silent joins/leaves and private errors. Discord is mocked in these tests; actual client rendering requires a human button click.

Queue cards no longer have a Community Games footer. Optional voice previews replace that space with an indented italic list, without an extra heading. A **Ping** button posts a separate invitation to eligible voice participants with a Join button; it has a shared five-minute cooldown and no private success receipt. They are off until explicitly enabled in a queue’s Voice presence settings.

The visibility deadline is disposable DO key-value state (`queueVisibilityDue`), reconstructed after restart if absent. Each queue's last activity (`queueActivity`) and refused-channel streaks (`channelRefusals`) are disposable KV entries too: no SQL migration. Losing them only pauses checks until the next activity, or restarts a refusal count. An additive `queue_publications.visibility_only` column identifies scheduled work so disabling the setting can cancel it without dropping player updates. Existing rows default to zero. SQL backups include the column; older backups restore with its default, and recovery copies remain read-only. Existing queue membership, matches, ratings and wallets are unchanged by deployment. Prefer a forward repair; before downgrading, drain pending visibility work and remove the new setting from saved configurations through a supported configuration change. Older binaries do not honor cancellation of scheduled work and cannot import backups containing the new column.

## Cost estimate for 50 communities

Checks follow use. Assume **one monitored channel per community**, a 30-day month, and a community that plays for **3 hours each evening**: checks run for those 3 hours plus the 6-hour tail, `9 × 4 = 36` checks a day.

- `50 × 30 × 36 = 54,000` periodic channel checks per month. A community that is not used for a day costs nothing that day; a community in use around the clock is bounded by the previous figure, `50 × 30 × 96 = 144,000`.
- Unchanged channels need one Discord GET and no message writes. Queues in the same channel share that GET. A replacement adds a fresh history check, POST and DELETE; normal interactions and recovery retries are additional.
- Routine polling uses **2 DO invocations per check** (the alarm and the shared rate-gate reservation): about 108,000 per month at this usage. Staging previously measured 4 per check, because every alarm fired twice; `tests/alarm-budget.test.ts` now fails if an alarm re-arms for already-due work.
- Staging measured about 0.1–0.4 seconds of active wall time for a completed check, but each check also held a cancelled duplicate open for about 5 seconds (the unreleased fetch timeout). Without that, at an assumed **0.5 seconds per check**, duration is `54,000 × 0.5 × 0.128 ≈ 3,500 GB-seconds/month` (9,200 for round-the-clock use). This is an estimate, not a billing statement.

The regression test drives a representative idle day for a community with two enabled queues in two channels. Before these changes it took 96 alarm invocations plus 96 overlapping duplicates and 192 Discord requests every day, indefinitely; now the day after the last activity takes 23 invocations and 46 requests (the 6-hour tail), and every following idle day takes none.

With otherwise available allowances, the periodic feature fits within the **$5/month Workers Paid baseline**, below the requested $10. SQLite reads/writes and these small records are expected to fit the included allocations at this scale. Multiple channels, high traffic, slow APIs, retries and unrelated account usage increase consumption. This is not a hard spending cap.

**The complete existing app cannot be promised below $10 solely by choosing this interval.** A continuously open outbound voice connection uses about 331,776 GB-seconds/month; the [voice preview](voice-preview.md) now holds it only while a voice-preview queue is in use. The AoE2 feed adds its own duration. Cloudflare rounds excess duration up to million-GB-second billing units at $12.50 each, so even a small overage can put the total above $10. Account for those services separately and review actual usage; no billing monitor or automatic game shutdown is introduced here.

Pricing checked 27 October 2026 (Pacific): [Durable Object pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/) and [Workers pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/). The included allowances are shared across the account.

## Match-start ordering and preparation settings

Each match-start queue publication stores its required match IDs in the additive `queue_publications.after_matches` column. Existing rows and older restored backups default to `[]`. Queue replacements wait for recorded match-message receipts, including when several matches start before delivery. New joins coalesce into the waiting publication without losing this ordering. A blocked match publication blocks its dependent queue publication without an idle retry loop; recovering the match card wakes the queue publication. Lost receipts retain the existing nonce and reconciliation behavior. Back up before deployment; older binaries cannot import backups containing the additional column. No player, match, rating or wallet tables change.

In **Website → Queues → your queue**, set **Check-in time limit (seconds)** (1–3600) and save. The **Require check-in** toggle remains independent. Turn off **Enable map selection / voting** to skip all map voting and picking for new matches; map pools and preferences remain saved for later reuse. These settings do not change a match already in progress. When the optional map vote-as-check-in setting is enabled, its map selection timer controls that combined stage, as labelled in the form.
